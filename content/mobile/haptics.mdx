A haptic is a small tap on the hand that says something happened. A good one
confirms what the eye already sees: the wheel landed on 12, the payment went
through. A bad one is a buzz that means nothing, and people switch it off.

Scrub the wheel in each mode.

<HapticPickerDemo />

Every frame fires on each pixel the wheel moves, so a fling becomes one long
buzz. None leaves a fast scrub with no sense of how far it went. Per value
plays one selection tick each time a new number crosses the line, and a
firmer impact when the wheel hits the end.

**Play a haptic only at a moment the screen also shows, and use the system pattern whose meaning matches it.**

## Use the named patterns

Both platforms ship a small vocabulary, and people learn it from the system
apps. Apple asks you to use the patterns "according to their documented
meanings", in three families:

- **Selection** while a value changes: pickers, sliders, segmented controls.
- **Impact** for a physical metaphor: something snaps into place or collides.
- **Notification** for the outcome of a task: success, warning or error.

<HapticVocabularyDemo />

| Moment | SwiftUI | Android View | Compose | Expo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value steps | `.selection` | `SEGMENT_TICK` | `SegmentTick` | `selectionAsync()` |
| Snap, or a drag crosses its threshold | `.impact(weight:)` | `GESTURE_THRESHOLD_ACTIVATE` | `GestureThresholdActivate` | `impactAsync(Light)` |
| Toggle | built into `Toggle` | `TOGGLE_ON` / `TOGGLE_OFF` | `ToggleOn` / `ToggleOff` | iOS `Switch` plays it; Android `Toggle_On` |
| Success | `.success` | `CONFIRM` | `Confirm` | `notificationAsync(Success)` |
| Failure | `.error` | `REJECT` | `Reject` | `notificationAsync(Error)` |

Android has no warning pattern and no impact weights. Its constants are named
for what happened, and each device decides how that feels.

## iPhone

- SwiftUI's `.sensoryFeedback(_:trigger:)` (iOS 17) plays when the trigger
  value changes. On iPhone only `.selection`, `.impact`, `.success`,
  `.warning`, `.error`, `.alignment` and `.pathComplete` play. `.increase`,
  `.decrease`, `.start`, `.stop` and `.levelChange` compile and stay silent;
  they're for the watch, the Mac or Vision Pro.
- `Toggle`, `Slider` and `Picker` already play their own haptics. Adding yours
  doubles them.
- In UIKit, call `prepare()` when the gesture begins, not right before firing.
  It wakes the Taptic Engine for a few seconds. iOS 27 deprecates
  `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator(style:)`; use `init(style:view:)` (iOS 17.5).

## Android

- `View.performHapticFeedback` needs no `VIBRATE` permission and respects the
  system touch-feedback setting. `CONFIRM` and `REJECT` arrived in API 30, the
  toggle, tick and threshold constants in API 34. Call them through
  `ViewCompat` so older phones get a fallback instead of nothing.
- Skip `Vibrator.vibrate()` for interface feedback. Google's rule: "Given the
  choice of buzzy haptics or no haptics for touch feedback, choose no
  haptics." A crisp click lasts 10 to 20 ms; a cheap motor rings for another
  20 to 50 ms after it.
- Compose's `LocalHapticFeedback` covers 13 of the 20 constants (Compose UI
  1.8+). Material 3's `Switch`, `Slider` and sheets play nothing, so add them.
  `combinedClickable(onLongClick = …)` already fires the long-press haptic, so
  don't add a second ([Long Press](/long-press)).

## React Native and Flutter

- expo-haptics has the iOS vocabulary: `selectionAsync()`,
  `impactAsync(Light | Medium | Heavy | Rigid | Soft)` and
  `notificationAsync(Success | Warning | Error)`. On Android,
  `performAndroidHapticsAsync(AndroidHaptics.Segment_Tick)` reaches the
  platform constants directly; it was broken before expo-haptics 57.0.2.
- Fire haptics from the JS thread. When the value lives in a Reanimated
  shared value, react to the index changing and hop over with `scheduleOnRN`.
- Flutter's `HapticFeedback.selectionClick()` maps to the iOS selection
  generator and to `CLOCK_TICK` on Android; the success and error
  notifications map to `CONFIRM` and `REJECT` on API 30+.

## How often

- On the value change, never per frame of a drag.
- On commit, not on touch-down for something that can still be cancelled.
- Not on every tap. A button's pressed state already answers the finger
  ([Touch Feedback](/touch-feedback)).
- Light for frequent events, stronger for rare ones. A heavy impact on every
  row stops meaning anything.

A haptic is always the second signal, never the only one. People turn system
haptics off, iOS plays none in Low Power Mode or while the camera is in use,
and on some Android phones light, medium and heavy feel identical. The
platform APIs follow the user's setting for you. If you play custom patterns through Core Haptics or `Vibrator`, add your own switch
in Settings, as Apple's guidelines ask: "Make haptics optional."
